Promises moved the wrong way in Feb-Mar 2018

Weekly promises fell from 27.7 to 22.9 days while the weekly late rate climbed to 26%. After Black Friday 2017 promises were widened and lateness fell. Why promises changed is not in the data.

Early warning: in-transit ageing leads late deliveries

Share of parcels handed to the carrier 10-30 days earlier and still undelivered, knowable each Monday. In 2018 it crossed 20% on 2018-02-19, 3 weeks before the late share of deliveries passed 10% (2018-03-12). Threshold set in-sample on two episodes; the May 2017 alarms preceded only a mild rise.

Promise guardrail backtest

Rule: when ageing is at or above 20%, add k days to every promise made that week (13 alarm weeks, 17,051 orders). +5 days would have turned 727 late orders on time, 12% of all late orders in the window. Assumes delivery is unchanged by the promise; conversion cost is not measurable here.

Lane Pareto: rank lanes by late orders, not late rate

SP -> SP and SP -> RJ carry 38% of late orders; 5 of 405 lanes carry half. SP -> RJ is peak-driven: 7.5% late in normal months, 37.7% in peaks.

Promise curve: what each extra day buys

SP -> AL is 75% on time at today's 32.9-day promise; +3 days gives 78%, +5 gives 84%, and reaching 93% needs +12 days. Meanwhile the median order arrives 13 days early: promises are mis-calibrated, not uniformly short.
