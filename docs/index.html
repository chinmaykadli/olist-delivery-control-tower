Where the delay sits: mostly the carrier, not the seller

Late orders spend 3 extra days waiting for the seller to hand over, but 21 extra days in transit. Seller penalties alone cannot fix lateness - the memo leads with this.

Late rate and review score by month

Window ends May 2018: later months are right-censored (their late orders were still in transit at the data cut-off). March 2018's 19% spike is real, not censoring.

Later delivery, lower reviews - with the survey-timing split

76% of late-order reviews were written BEFORE the parcel arrived (the survey goes out when the estimated date passes). Written after delivery, late orders average about 3.4 stars, not 2.2. Recommendation: trigger the survey on delivery confirmation, not on the estimate.

Seller concentration is real but modest

Among 511 sellers with 30+ orders, the worst 10% run a 19.2% late rate vs 6.8% for the rest - but they cause only 13% of all late orders. Persistence check: the worst 2017 decile cooled from 17.1% to 11.5% in 2018 (still above the 5-8% field).

Early-warning score: honest lift on the 2018 holdout

Logistic model on trailing-90-day dispatch, breach, late rate, review, cancellations and volume; trained on 2017, tested on Jan-May 2018. Top-20 monthly queue: 26% hit rate vs 18% base (1.42x lift). Positive but weak - good enough for a weekly conversation queue, never for automatic throttling.

Worst seller-state to customer-state routes (100+ orders)

Long-haul routes out of Sao Paulo to the North-East dominate late deliveries. Memo recommendation: recalibrate delivery promises on these routes to the historical 80th percentile.
