Lateness is a peak problem first

Peak months (red; rule: marketplace late rate of 10% or more) hold 23% of orders but 51% of late orders. In peaks the average promise was 23.6 days against 25.3 normally, while transit took 12.5 days against 8.7.

Where the delay sits: mostly with the carrier

Late orders wait 3 extra days for handover but spend 21 extra days in transit. Seller penalties alone cannot fix lateness.

Later delivery, lower reviews, but most late reviews come before the parcel

72% of late-order reviews were answered before delivery (the survey is sent when the estimate passes). Late orders reviewed after arrival average 3.69 stars (n=1,700) against 4.28 on time. Customers who wait to answer may differ from those who do not.

Seller concentration is real but modest

Ranked by late-order COUNT (chart), the top 10% of these sellers carry 50% of their late orders. Ranked by late RATE, among 581 sellers with 30+ orders the worst 10% run 17.2% late against 6.2% for the rest, and account for 13% of all late orders. Year-on-year persistence is moderate (Spearman 0.28 across 260 sellers).

Who to call: a one-line sort is as good as the model

Jan-Jul 2018 holdout. The queue (A: sort by trailing late rate, chosen on 2017 only) hits 34% (95% CI 26% to 42%) against a 21% base, 1.59x random. The 5-feature logistic model hits 33%. Good enough to decide who gets a call; never for throttling.
